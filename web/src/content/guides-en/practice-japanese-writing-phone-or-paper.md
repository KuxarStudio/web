A common question when [learning to write Japanese by hand](/en/guides/learn-to-write-japanese-by-hand/): do I practice on my phone or on paper? The short answer is that **each one solves a different part**, and the most practical approach is to combine them.

## What paper gives you

- **Control of proportion.** On paper you see the whole character, its balance and the space between strokes. That matters most for kanji with many strokes.
- **Hand movement and pressure.** Stroke endings (stopping, lifting, sweeping) are learned with a pencil or pen.
- **No distractions**, and a visible record of your progress.

Its limit: nobody tells you whether the order or direction of a stroke is right. If you get it wrong, you repeat the mistake without knowing.

## What the phone gives you

- **Immediate correction.** An app with stroke recognition can tell you right away that the order or direction is wrong.
- **Short sessions** at any moment: on the commute, during a break.
- **Spaced review and daily challenges**, with nothing to prepare.

Its limit: the screen is small, there is no paper friction, and tracing with a finger is not identical to using a pen.

## What the research says

There is evidence that handwriting helps you remember characters better than typing (a small study by Longcamp and colleagues in *Human Movement Science*, with 12 adults; [here is the summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167945706000649)). What we have not found is a study that directly compares tracing with a finger on a screen against writing on paper, so we will not claim that one is better than the other.

## A combined routine

1. **On your phone:** trace the character with order and direction correction, in sessions of 5 to 10 minutes.
2. **On paper:** write from memory the characters from that session and watch the proportions. A squared notebook helps.
3. **The next day:** review yesterday's characters on the phone before adding new ones.

It is the same "look, trace, write from memory" logic we describe in [how to learn hiragana by writing](/en/guides/how-to-learn-hiragana-by-writing/).
