Some kana characters trip up every beginner. The good news: they are almost always told apart by **the direction of one stroke or the number of strokes**, which is exactly what you learn when you practice with proper order. It is one of the strongest reasons to [write Japanese by hand](/en/guides/learn-to-write-japanese-by-hand/) instead of just recognizing it.

## Katakana

- **シ and ツ.** In シ the two short strokes are stacked one above the other and the long stroke **rises** from bottom-left to top-right. In ツ the short strokes sit side by side and the long one **falls** from top-right to bottom-left.
- **ソ and ン.** In ソ both strokes go down; the long one falls to the left. In ン the long stroke starts low and **rises** to the right.
- **ク and ワ.** The first stroke of ク is a short diagonal; the first stroke of ワ is a short vertical line.
- **ウ and ワ.** ウ has three strokes (it starts with a short mark on top); ワ has two.
- **ノ and メ.** ノ is a single stroke; メ is two crossing strokes.

## Hiragana

- **ぬ and め.** ぬ ends with a loop in its tail; め ends without one.
- **わ, れ and ね.** They share the first stroke. ね ends in a loop, れ in an open tail that kicks outward, and わ in a curve that turns back inward.
- **る and ろ.** る closes with a loop; ろ does not.
- **は and ほ.** ほ has one more horizontal line than は.
- **さ and き.** き has two horizontal lines; さ has just one.

## How to practice them

1. **Group them.** Write the pair one next to the other to lock in the difference.
2. **Watch the direction.** Say "rises" or "falls" out loud while you trace シ/ツ and ソ/ン.
3. **Use stroke order.** Each character has a specific order; see [the stroke order rules](/en/guides/japanese-stroke-order-rules/).
4. **Review them separately** from new rows, because they are the ones that get forgotten most.

## If you are just starting

If you do not yet know the difference between the two syllabaries, begin with [hiragana vs katakana](/en/guides/hiragana-vs-katakana/), and follow the plan in [learning hiragana by writing](/en/guides/how-to-learn-hiragana-by-writing/).

In [Kaku!](/en/kaku/) stroke correction checks order and direction, which is precisely what separates these pairs: a stroke drawn in the wrong direction shows up right away.
